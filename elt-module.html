Problem statement

Many B1 students feel high foreign language anxiety in spoken interaction assessments. Participation is often unequal and proficiency levels differ. This module reduces those barriers with structured UDL scaffolding and digital interactivity.

Learning objective

Students will show functional pragmatic competence (giving advice and sustaining informal discussion), strategic communication skills and target vocabulary retention through gamified digital scenarios.

Academic articulation

Task-Based Language Teaching (TBLT) · Universal Design for Learning (UDL) · CEFR B1 spoken interaction descriptors · interactive digital media (Canva / LabINNOVA tools).

UDL features in this module

🔊 Dual-mode prompts (text + audio/video) · 🎨 high-contrast phrase banks (yellow on navy) · 🔀 flexible response pathways (say, write or choose) · ✅ real-time formative feedback · 🔠 text size and light/dark controls at the top.

Target vocabulary & phrases

You should…Why don’t you…?If I were you, I’d…How about…?I’d suggest…That’s a good idea!What do you think?Really? Tell me more.

📖 Reading

1. Advice Match Game

Read each message from a friend. Choose the best piece of advice. Mistakes are fine: you get a hint and can try again!

🎯 Learning objectiveI can recognise polite advice phrases in short texts, so I feel less anxious when I need to answer a friend in a real conversation.

2. B1 Reading Practice Open resource

Choose one short text on a topic you like. Read it twice: first for the main idea, then to find 3 new words. Write them in your word list.

Open British Council B1 Reading ↗

🎯 Learning objectiveI can choose a text at my own level and pace, building vocabulary in a stress-free way that I can use in speaking tasks.

🎧 Listening

1. Listen & Learn: strategies Open resource

A B1 teacher talks about how to improve listening. Do the preparation task, listen, then do the exercises. Use the transcript if you need it.

Open the lesson ↗

🎯 Learning objectiveI can learn strategies to understand speakers, so that I feel more confident when I have to respond in a conversation.

2. Free listening library Open resource

Pick a B1 conversation (friends, phone calls, meetings) or a short BBC podcast. Listen once without the text, once with it.

B1 Listening lessons ↗ · BBC 6 Minute English ↗

🎯 Learning objectiveI can listen to real informal talk at my own speed and notice how speakers react and keep a conversation going.

3. My Listening Plan Self-check

🎯 Learning objectiveI can plan how I listen, which gives me control and reduces anxiety before speaking with a partner.

✍️ Writing

1. Reply to a Friend Game

Your friend writes: “I feel very nervous when I speak English in class. What should I do?”

Write 40–60 words. Tap a phrase to add it.

0 words

🎯 Learning objectiveI can write advice with target phrases first. Practising in writing prepares me to say the same ideas aloud with less fear.

2. B1 Writing Practice Open resource

Choose a short writing lesson (emails, messages, informal texts). Compare your answer with the model.

Open British Council B1 Writing ↗

🎯 Learning objectiveI can use models and feedback to improve my informal writing, so every learner has a fair way to show what they know.

🗣️ Speaking

1. Advice Role-Play Game

Work in pairs. Press the button to get a problem. Partner A shares it, Partner B gives advice. Then switch roles, so both of you speak!

Press the button to start.

Choose your way to answer:

If I were you, I’d…Why don’t you…?You should…How about…?What do you think?That’s a good idea!
🎯 Learning objectiveI can give advice and keep an informal talk going with a partner, using the way of answering that makes me feel safe. Both partners take turns, so participation is equal.

2. Watch & Repeat: Giving advice Open video · stretch

Watch Paul, Bob and Noelia and repeat the useful phrases. This lesson is slightly above B1, so use it as a challenge.

Open the video lesson ↗

🎯 Learning objectiveI can copy natural advice phrases from a model and practise them privately first, which lowers anxiety before the group task.

3. Peer Feedback Stars Self & peer

After the role-play, tell your partner one thing they did well and one tip.

I liked how you…Next time, try…Your advice was clear!
🎯 Learning objectiveI can give and receive kind feedback, which builds a supportive group and helps every proficiency level take part.
